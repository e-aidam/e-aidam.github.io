Hyperthermia Gene Set Enrichment Analysis

End-to-end RNA-seq and GSEA workflow for whole-blood samples after exposure to temperature stress.

Background

Can whole-body hyperthermia alter biological pathways implicated in depression?

Whole-body hyperthermia (WBH) is a medical procedure that intentionally raises body temperature and has been investigated as a potential treatment for depression. Previous research found that heat shock, immune-response, and interleukin-6 pathways were enriched following WBH, but the effects of treatment on other depression-relevant pathways remained less clear.

We analyzed whole-blood RNA-seq data to investigate whether WBH alters expression across serotonin, dopamine, intermediate signaling, and heat-shock pathways, moving beyond individual genes to identify coordinated changes in biologically related gene sets.

Project Goals

Reproduce Published Findings

Reanalyze whole-blood RNA-seq data from a published WBH study and compare pathway-level results across differential-expression methods.

Implement GSEA From Scratch

Build a custom Gene Set Enrichment Analysis pipeline to calculate enrichment scores, normalized enrichment scores, and permutation-based significance.

Investigate Depression-Relevant Pathways

Test whether WBH produces coordinated expression changes in serotonin, dopamine, intermediate signaling, and heat-shock pathways.

Control Statistical Error

Apply Benjamini-Hochberg and Storey multiple-testing corrections to distinguish exploratory pathway signals from statistically supported findings.

Pipeline

From sequencing reads to pathway scores.

The analysis moves from count data to ranked treatment contrasts, then asks whether predefined gene sets appear unusually early or late in that ranked list.

01 FASTQ

Download paired sequencing files by SRR accession.

02 QC

Run FastQC to inspect read quality before alignment.

03 STAR

Align reads and produce gene-level count outputs.

04 DESeq2

Model WBH versus sham and rank genes by log2 fold change.

05 Custom GSEA

Compute running ES, NES, empirical p-values, and adjusted p-values in R.

06 Broad GSEA

Compare custom results with preranked desktop analyses.

Metric Definition

NES measures the strength and direction of pathway enrichment.

The Normalized Enrichment Score (NES) compares a gene set’s observed enrichment score with scores expected under phenotype permutations. Normalization allows enrichment to be compared across gene sets while preserving direction: positive NES values indicate enrichment toward genes elevated in WBH, while negative values indicate enrichment toward the opposite end of the ranked expression profile.
